# Cornerstone Guide Source Notes

## Powered Hiking Assist

Hypershell positions its outdoor exoskeleton line for hiking, mountain exploration, cycling, photography, ski touring, rescue, inspection, and security. Outdoor Life’s 2025 field comparison of Hypershell Pro X and Dnsys X1 Carbon reported useful movement sensing but also flagged altered gait, discomfort, higher foot impact, and a recalibration period after use. The guide should present the category as emerging high-consideration technology, never as medical treatment.

Sources: Hypershell Global, https://hypershell.tech/en-us/; Laura Lancaster, Outdoor Life, “Can an Exoskeleton Help You Bag a Peak?,” June 27, 2025, https://www.outdoorlife.com/gear/can-an-exoskeleton-for-hiking-help-you-bag-a-peak/.

## Portable Power

EcoFlow presents DELTA Pro 3 as a portable power system for home, RV, and off-grid use, with expansion and solar/car/outlet charging options. The guide will avoid publishing current pricing, runtime estimates, or brand-comparison verdicts without current source checks. It will focus on the ownership system: input, distribution, storage, carry weight, cable management, warranty, and intended load.

Source: EcoFlow, “DELTA Pro 3 Portable Power Station,” https://us.ecoflow.com/products/delta-pro-3-portable-power-station.

## Satellite Communication

Garmin’s public product material positions inReach Messenger Plus as an SOS satellite communicator with smartphone-paired messaging features. The live product page did not expose readable text in the research browser, so the guide will cite Garmin’s category and press materials while keeping safety claims limited: a satellite communicator is a communication layer and not a substitute for route planning, weather awareness, training, or early decision-making.

Sources: Garmin, “inReach Messenger Plus,” https://www.garmin.com/en-US/p/1191310/; Garmin, “inReach Satellite Communicators,” https://www.garmin.com/en-US/c/outdoor-recreation/satellite-communicators/.
