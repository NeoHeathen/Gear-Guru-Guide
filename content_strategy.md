# Gear Guru Guide: Launch Content Structure

## Refined Founder Story

> **Gear Guru Guide began with a simple belief: a life outdoors does not have an expiration date.**

I am an avid wilderness lover, and I created this project for friends who feel the same pull toward wild places—and who want to keep answering it as technology evolves. From navigation and off-grid power to powered mobility systems, Gear Guru Guide examines tools that may make an outing safer, more capable, or more sustainable. The goal is not to replace the wild; it is to help more people stay connected to it on their own terms.

## Recommended Site Sections

| Section | Purpose | Launch-ready content |
|---|---|---|
| **Home / Current Brief** | Give visitors a reason to trust and explore the brand. | Hero, current category watch, three editorial principles, email capture. |
| **Buying Guides** | Answer high-intent purchase questions without pretending every item suits every person. | Exoskeletons, satellite communicators, power stations, expedition shelters, creator equipment. |
| **Field Notes** | Publish short, timely observations that build topical authority. | Product announcements, new-tech explainers, packing-system notes, field-testing methodology. |
| **The Watchlist** | Make the first 20 items discoverable while research is underway. | Model, category, decision focus, status, and a launch-update button; no unverified live price or rating. |
| **How We Evaluate** | Explain why readers should trust the verdict. | Context, claim audit, system cost, carry weight, repair/service, ownership friction, safety boundaries. |
| **About / Why This Exists** | Carry the founder’s wilderness-first perspective. | The refined story above, paired with the “technology should extend the experience” promise. |
| **The Signal** | Build an owned audience outside social platforms. | Weekly or twice-monthly field brief with one decision framework and selected new gear. |
| **Disclosures & Policies** | Protect readers and create affiliate transparency. | Affiliate disclosure, safety statement, editorial policy, privacy policy, terms, and contact page. |

## Trust and Disclosure Framework

The language below is a practical starting point, not legal advice. Before publishing, the final terms, privacy policy, and cookie language should be reviewed for the actual business location, analytics tools, email provider, and affiliate programs in use.

| Placement | Suggested plain-language text | Why it belongs there |
|---|---|---|
| **Sitewide footer** | “Gear Guru Guide is an independent editorial project. Gear is not a substitute for training, sound judgment, weather awareness, or applicable land and safety rules.” | Sets the safety and editorial boundary on every page. |
| **Top of any affiliate guide** | “Disclosure: This guide may contain affiliate links. If you buy through a qualifying link, Gear Guru Guide may earn a commission at no additional cost to you. Our assessment framework stays the same whether or not a product earns a commission.” | Transparent connection between commercial links and editorial content. |
| **Product-price modules** | “Price and availability can change. Check the retailer’s current page before purchasing.” | Avoids stale commerce information and reduces implied price guarantees. |
| **Exoskeleton / assistive technology guides** | “Outdoor exoskeletons are consumer products, not medical devices. They do not replace professional medical advice, training, or personal judgment. Review manufacturer warnings and use only as directed.” | Prevents implied medical claims and keeps safety responsibility clear. |
| **Testing and claim policy** | “We label manufacturer specifications, independent observations, and our own field impressions separately. A product’s inclusion is not a guarantee of suitability or performance.” | Makes the editorial standard concrete. |
| **AI-assisted visual disclosure, if used** | “Some visual concepts may be AI-assisted illustrations and are not evidence of a product’s real-world performance.” | Prevents synthetic visuals from appearing to be field-test proof. |

## Operating Standards for Each Review

Every long-form review should begin by naming the intended user, terrain, trip duration, budget range, and alternatives. It should then separate manufacturer claims from independently supported observations, identify what has and has not been tested, and include the true ownership cost: accessories, subscriptions, consumables, charging, maintenance, and support. A closing “Skip if…” section is important for high-dollar gear because it protects reader trust better than a blanket recommendation.

## Affiliate Data Boundary

For the launch period, use owner-generated Associate links only after Amazon accepts the site. Do not publish live prices, star ratings, review counts, or retailer-hosted images manually. Amazon’s current Creators API documentation says access requires Associates enrollment, registration, credentials, and at least 10 qualifying sales in the prior 30 days for PA API access through Creators API.[1] The website should use editorial product cards until that eligibility is confirmed.
