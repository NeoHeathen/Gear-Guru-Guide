# Additional Exoskeleton Research Notes

## Skip MO/GO

Skip’s official site presents **MO/GO** as wearable technology developed in collaboration with Arc’teryx and available to reserve or pre-order. The manufacturer includes an explicit notice that MO/GO is not a medical device and has not been cleared by the FDA. Gear Guru Guide may include it as a premium **outdoor mobility research** entry, but any article must preserve this boundary and must not frame it as medical treatment or make performance claims beyond clearly attributed manufacturer statements.

Source: [Skip — Move More with Movewear](https://www.skipwithjoy.com/)

## WIRobotics WIM S

WIRobotics’ official site lists **WIM S** as the next generation of its WIM wearable robot line. The product is a distinct consumer wearable-mobility research track. The public site also contains rehabilitation-oriented testimonials, so it should be classified as **editorial research only** for Gear Guru Guide until a suitable outdoor use case and affiliate pathway are confirmed. Do not make medical or rehabilitation claims in catalog copy.

Source: [WIRobotics — WIM S](https://corp.wirobotics.com/en)

## VIGX π Series

VIGX’s official product page identifies **VIGX π Plus** as an “Intelligent Powered Sport Outdoor Exoskeleton.” Its catalog also lists the VIGX π Wearable Exoskeleton Sports Assist Robot, Ant-H1 Pro AI-Powered Wearable Exoskeleton Walking Assist Robot, and ANT-P1 Sports Enhanced. These can be added as distinct outdoor-mobility research entries, but commercial-path status remains **editorial research only** until a documented creator or publisher program is confirmed.

Source: [VIGX π Plus product page](https://vigx.ai/products/%CF%80-plus-sports-assist-robot)
