# Gear Guru Guide: First Four-Week Faceless Video Sprint

This launch plan creates **four YouTube flagship videos** and **eight short-form cutdowns**. Each short is designed as a self-contained 20–30 second video for Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, and TikTok. The longer YouTube video should use original narration, properly licensed or self-shot field material where available, and selected AI-assisted atmospheric b-roll. Higgsfield supports prompt control across subject, style, camera, lighting, mood, aspect ratio, references, shot lists, output length, and variants; the prompts below are therefore structured shot by shot.[1] [2]

> **Creative rule:** Use AI video to illustrate concepts, motion, terrain, and mood. Do not present generated visuals as a real field test, do not replicate branded product geometry or logos, and do not make medical, endurance, safety, price, or performance claims that the editorial piece cannot support.

## Workflow for Every Video

| Step | Deliverable | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Research brief** | One source-backed thesis, the intended audience, manufacturer claims clearly marked, and a “what we do not know” line. | Day 1 |
| **2. Script** | 650–900 words for the YouTube piece; 55–75 words for each short. | Day 2 |
| **3. Higgsfield shots** | Generate 5–7 individual 5–8 second clips from the relevant shot prompt. Select the two strongest variants per shot. | Day 3 |
| **4. Edit and label** | Cut to an original voiceover. Use captions and a visible “Illustrative AI-assisted visual” label where generated b-roll could otherwise appear to depict a real product test. | Day 4 |
| **5. Publish and repurpose** | Post the long video, one short on launch day, and the second short 48–72 hours later. Link to the related Gear Guru Guide article—not directly to an affiliate product page. | Days 5–7 |

## Video 1: Outdoor Exoskeletons

### YouTube package

| Element | Direction |
|---|---|
| **Working title** | *Can Outdoor Exoskeletons Keep Us Exploring Longer?* |
| **Audience** | Experienced hikers, photographers, aging-but-active outdoors people, and gear-curious viewers evaluating a high-dollar emerging category. |
| **Promise** | Explain the decision factors without assuming every hiker needs powered assistance. |
| **Opening narration** | “The next big trail tool may not go in your pack. It may strap around your hips. But before we call powered hiking the future, we need to ask a better question: does this technology actually belong on your trail?” |
| **Key editorial guardrail** | State that consumer outdoor exoskeletons are not medical devices and should not be framed as treatment or a substitute for conditioning, training, or judgment. |
| **Call to action** | “Get the free Gear Guru Guide field brief for the full powered-mobility decision checklist.” |

### Higgsfield shot prompt

```text
Create a 9:16 cinematic faceless outdoor-technology video for a skeptical, premium gear publication.
Subject: an unbranded, generic powered hiking-assist device worn over a hiker’s outer clothing; only gloved hands, boots, waist, and legs are visible. Do not show a face, brand marks, readable product labels, or an exact real product design.
Composition: generate these six short shots as separate clips: (1) close low-angle boot steps on wet granite at dawn, (2) gloved hands tightening a waist harness over a technical shell, (3) side profile of the lower body walking uphill on a broad alpine trail, (4) macro of a generic battery indicator glowing softly, (5) boots pausing at a trail map beside a pack, (6) wide rear silhouette moving slowly toward a mountain ridgeline.
Style: premium outdoor documentary, deep forest shadows, warm paper highlights, acid-lime equipment accents, cool morning mist, tactile 35mm film grain, natural restrained motion.
Camera: low tracking shot, locked detail close-ups, one slow crane-back wide shot; realistic walking gait and terrain contact.
Text/content to render: no text.
Constraints: vertical 9:16, 6–8 seconds per shot, no impossible biomechanics, no levitation, no product logos, no claim graphics, no face.
Avoid: science-fiction armor, combat aesthetics, medical imagery, miraculous assistance, exaggerated speed, brand imitation, distorted limbs.
```

### Short-form cutdowns

| Short | Hook | Voiceover core | Visual selection |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1A: Robot legs at the trailhead** | “This may be the most interesting—and most misunderstood—new trail gear.” | “Powered hike assist is real consumer tech now. But the important questions are fit, terrain, fatigue, battery logistics, and what happens when you switch it off.” | Shots 1, 2, 3, 6. |
| **1B: Three questions first** | “Before you spend four figures on powered hike assist, ask these three things.” | “What terrain will you actually use it on? What is the total carry system, including batteries? And do you have a reason beyond wanting the newest thing?” | Shots 2, 4, 5, 6. |

## Video 2: Portable Power Systems

### YouTube package

| Element | Direction |
|---|---|
| **Working title** | *The Real Cost of Going Off-Grid: Portable Power Systems Explained* |
| **Audience** | Overlanders, camp creators, RV owners, and remote-work campers considering expensive battery systems. |
| **Opening narration** | “A big battery is easy to buy. Building a power system you can actually transport, recharge, trust, and live with is the hard part.” |
| **Key editorial guardrail** | Do not represent AI-generated power equipment as a real brand or quote runtime, capacity, charging speed, price, or safety specifications without the current manufacturer source. |
| **Call to action** | “Download the field brief for the five-line power-system worksheet.” |

### Higgsfield shot prompt

```text
Create a 16:9 cinematic faceless outdoor power-system video for a premium field-guide publication.
Subject: unbranded portable battery station, folding solar panel, camp table, cable management, and gloved hands in a quiet high-desert campsite; no people’s faces and no readable labels.
Composition: generate five separate 6–8 second clips: (1) slow sunrise across a solar panel beside a tent, (2) gloved hands connecting an unbranded cable to a battery station, (3) a close-up of a simple non-readable power display with soft ambient light, (4) a wide static scene of a camp workstation under a vehicle awning, (5) hands packing cables into a weatherproof pouch.
Style: restrained high-end product documentary, warm desert stone, graphite equipment, deep olive shadows, practical natural light, 35mm grain.
Camera: slow slider, overhead detail, one wide locked composition; realistic cable behavior and safe equipment handling.
Text/content to render: no text.
Constraints: horizontal 16:9, no logos, no readable display numbers, no sparks, no unsafe use, no product brand imitation.
Avoid: impossible energy effects, electric arcs, luxury-ad clichés, brand marks, fake specifications.
```

### Short-form cutdowns

| Short | Hook | Voiceover core | Visual selection |
|---|---|---|
| **2A: Power station math** | “The biggest mistake with portable power is buying the battery before defining the system.” | “Start with what you need to run, how long you need to run it, how you will recharge, and whether you can actually move the whole setup.” | Shots 1–4. |
| **2B: The question buyers skip** | “Can you repair, recharge, and carry this power system when the trip stops being convenient?” | “Big capacity is only one line on the checklist. The other lines are weight, input options, cables, storage, and support.” | Shots 2, 4, 5. |

## Video 4: The High-Dollar Gear Decision Framework

### YouTube package

| Element | Direction |
|---|---|

| **Audience** | Anyone about to buy an expensive outdoor system: powered mobility, camera gear, portable power, shelter, navigation, or overlanding equipment. |

| **Call to action** | “Subscribe to get the one-page Gear Guru Guide decision worksheet.” |

### Higgsfield shot prompt

```text
Create a 16:9 premium faceless editorial montage about making smart high-dollar outdoor gear decisions.
Subject: generic high-end outdoor equipment arranged on a warm paper map: hiking boots, a compact navigation device, an unbranded battery, folded rain shell, camera body without marks, and notebook; gloved hands move one item at a time.
Composition: generate five separate clips: (1) overhead of the full gear system on a topographic map, (2) hand weighing a compact item in a small travel scale, (3) close detail of cable, battery, and repair tape in a pouch, (4) hand writing “need / carry / charge / repair / skip” in a notebook, (5) slow pull-back from packed gear at a trailhead before first light.

Camera: overhead, macro, slow pull-back; deliberate unhurried movement.
Text/content to render: no text; the notebook writing should be abstract and not readable.
Constraints: horizontal 16:9, no logos, no sale imagery, no face, no fake product specifications.

```

| Short | Hook | Voiceover core | Visual selection |

## Editing and Publishing Checklist

Before publishing every video, use a title and thumbnail that reflect the actual conclusion, add captions, and include the site’s affiliate disclosure only where affiliate links are present. The description should link to the matching Gear Guru Guide article and state whether visuals are illustrative. Avoid publishing direct Amazon links until the Associates account and tracking ID are active; the site content should first create trust and original editorial value.
